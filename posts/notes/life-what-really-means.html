An unbalanced life is rarely a decision. No one sets out to neglect their health, their relationships or their own interests. It happens slowly, one area getting less attention until months have passed and you realise it has been missing. The problem is that you cannot manage what you do not track, and most people never define what a balanced life means for them in the first place.

I wanted something personal rather than what society tells you should matter, and something I could plan, schedule and check. Here is a simple framework I use, and it spells the word itself, LIFE, which makes it easy to remember: Love, Interests, Fun, Exploration.

Love

Everything starts with love, and I do not mean romance. I mean self-love: actually looking after myself. Body, health, mind, emotions, and the way I treat myself day to day. This comes first, because people rarely get the best of you when you are run down. Everything else sits on top of it.

Interests

The things that genuinely pull you in. The hobbies and pursuits you would do whether or not anyone was watching or paying. I have a saying: your interests are what make you interesting. A life with no interests of its own is just work and obligation.

Fun

This is the easiest one to defer forever. There is always a reason to postpone it. Spending your whole life building toward some day that may never come is not much of a life. So I do not defer fun. I schedule it.

Exploration

This is about staying curious. Reading, learning, meeting new people, seeing the world. There is always more out there than the small patch you already know.

How you can make this work

The framework only works if you check it. At the end of each week I ask one question: how was my LIFE this week? Then I score each of the four out of five. It takes a few minutes. Did I look after myself? Did I pursue my interests? Did I have any fun? Did I learn or see something new? I keep a diary of what I am doing under each one, so the scores are grounded in something real. A low score shows where you fell short, so you can add more the following week. Some weeks will not be balanced, and that is fine. You are not chasing a perfect scorecard, just trying to catch a gap while it is still a week, before it quietly becomes a year.
