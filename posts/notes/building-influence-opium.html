Influence has a bad reputation, mostly because people confuse it with manipulation. But influence is just leverage: the ability to move things in a direction you believe in. Go a whole career without it and you stay stuck, doing good work that never moves anything, while less capable people get their way instead. In any environment, that leverage comes from a small number of places.

Here is a simple framework I use, called OPIUM. There are five sources of influence, and almost nobody has all of them. Look closely at the most influential people and you will find they each dominate one or two, and let those pull the rest toward them: Opportunity, People, Information, Utility, Money.

O: Opportunity

Some people's power is that they can change your life with a single yes. Jay-Z can pluck an unknown artist and turn them into a household name. A director like Scorsese or Spielberg can cast someone nobody has heard of and make them a star overnight. Oprah could put a book on her list and make it a bestseller. When you control access to the opportunities people badly want, everyone trying to rise has to come through you. You are the door, and that is influence.

P: People

Other people's power is simply who they know. This is the currency of politics: a president is influential less for what they can personally do than for the coalition they can pick up the phone and move. The best-connected person in any industry makes things happen without lifting anything themselves: one introduction, one favour called in. Your network is borrowed leverage, and the people who build it deliberately end up at the centre of everything.

I: Information

Then there are people whose edge is knowing what others do not. Michael Bloomberg built a fortune, and a political career, selling the financial world the information it could not operate without. Warren Buffett's advantage was never a secret formula. It was reading more, and more carefully, than everyone else. The person with the clearest picture of what is really going on quietly shapes decisions everyone else is only guessing at.

U: Utility

The most honest form of influence is being irreplaceably good. LeBron James and Cristiano Ronaldo do not need anyone's permission. They are the value, and entire teams, leagues and businesses rearrange themselves around them. Sam Altman's pull in Silicon Valley came from years of being useful, building, advising, running Y Combinator, until he was wired into everything. When you are the best at something people need, you cannot be ignored. You matter because you actually deliver.

M: Money

And then there is the bluntest lever of all. When Elon Musk wants something built, bought, or changed, capital makes it move: companies, rockets, an entire social network bought outright. Command over resources, capital, budget, who gets paid, is the most transferable kind of power there is. It is not the deepest form of influence, and rarely the most admired, but pretending it is not power is naive.

How you can make this work

Notice the pattern. Dominate one source and it tends to pull the others in. Jay-Z turned opportunity into money. Athletes turn utility into a network. Politicians trade people for information and back again. You do not have to be born rich or connected. Pick the source you can realistically own, the skill you can become the best at, the information you can master, or the people you can bring together, and go deep on that one. None of this is a personality you are born with. Influence is leverage, and these are the five places it comes from.
