Longevity means the length of a life. More recently the word has taken on a broader meaning: the effort to live longer and to slow the effects of ageing. It has grown from a narrow area of research into a field of its own.

That field is expanding fast. There is a lot of money going into ageing research, much of it from technology investors and wealthy individuals. Around it has grown an industry of supplements, blood tests, wearables and protocols. Some of this is backed by good evidence. A lot of it is not. For anyone trying to make sensible decisions, the hard part is separating what is proven from what is being sold.

I look at longevity as a risk problem, because that is my background. I spent twenty years managing risk, and I started paying attention to my own health after a blood pressure reading came back higher than it should be. My view is that living longer is no longer the main issue. In most developed countries, reaching old age is now normal rather than rare. The questions that matter more are whether you stay healthy through those extra years, and whether your money lasts. I treat these as two things to manage: healthspan and wealthspan.

Healthspan

Lifespan is how long you live. Healthspan is how long you stay healthy while you live. The difference matters, because the two are often far apart. On average, people spend around their final decade in poor health, dealing with chronic conditions. Much of what causes that, such as heart disease and type 2 diabetes, is linked to everyday behaviour rather than fixed at birth. That makes it something you can act on. The aim is to keep healthspan closer to lifespan, so more of the extra years are worth having.

Wealthspan

Wealthspan is how long your money lasts, and whether it keeps up with a longer life. It gets less attention than health, but it matters just as much. If people are living into their 80s and beyond, their savings and income need to cover more years than earlier generations planned for. Good health does not help much if the money runs out first.

So when I write about longevity, I am writing about two things: staying healthy for as long as possible, and making sure the money lasts as long as you do. Most of the decisions involved build up over time, so starting earlier makes them easier. I will write about what I am doing on both, and where I get it wrong.
