For a long time I thought the person leading a conversation was the one doing most of the talking. That is backwards. The people who quietly run a room are usually the ones asking the questions, not answering them. Talk too much and you lose the room without noticing you have lost it.

Leading a conversation does not mean steering it toward yourself. It means making the other person feel like the most interesting person in the room, and letting the exchange go somewhere useful along the way. That is a little counterintuitive, which is why most people get it wrong.

Here is a simple framework I use for leading conversations, and for leading people. It spells LEAD: Listening, Emotions, Acknowledge, Digging. Two of them you manage inside yourself, and two you show toward the other person.

L: Listening

Most of us listen with a response already loaded, waiting for our turn. People feel that. The shift is to listen only to understand, and let your next line come from what they actually said, not from what you prepared while they were still talking.

Listening is the input. Get this wrong and nothing else works.

E: Emotions

This one is about you, not them. If you get defensive, impatient, or need to be right, the conversation stops being about the other person. Managing your own reactions, staying calm, not taking the bait, not correcting on reflex, is what keeps you in control.

You cannot listen well to someone if you are busy reacting to them.

A: Acknowledge

Listening is what you take in. Acknowledging is what you give back. It is the signal that shows the other person you heard them: reflecting what they said, naming it, holding eye contact, remembering the small thing they mentioned last time.

Someone can listen in silence and still feel cold. Acknowledgement is what makes them feel understood, and once a person feels understood they open up, which is what makes the next step work.

D: Digging

This is what separates leading a conversation from receiving one. Be curious. Ask the open question, "what made you get into that?", and then follow the thread. "Say more about that." Let them talk. Whoever asks the questions sets the direction.

Digging is how you take a flat exchange and find the part that matters to them.

The test

The simplest test of a conversation well led: does the other person walk away feeling a little more interesting, and a little more understood, than before? Do that consistently and people seek you out. It costs nothing but attention.

I am still worse at this than I would like. But treating conversation as something you lead, with Listening, Emotions, Acknowledge, and Digging, rather than something that happens to you, has changed how most of my days go.
