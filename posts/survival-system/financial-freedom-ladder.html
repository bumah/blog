The four levels of financial freedom, and how to climb each one.

Most of the money advice we get on financial freedom skips one question: where are you now? It focuses on building income streams and growing investments. But financial freedom is not one decision. There are levels to it, and each level changes what you and your money are supposed to be doing.

Let me break down the four levels from the bottom up.

Security
Freedom & protection
Surplus
Saving & investing
Stability
Debt control and emergency fund
Survival
Covering the bills

Level 1: Survival

Here your income roughly matches your expenses. You have little or no savings, and most likely you're living with a lot of debt. The job here is getting some breathing room. Cut what you can, and stop the debt from climbing. Nothing else really matters yet. Not investing, not pensions, none of it. Any advice about stocks or index funds is useless to someone at this level. Your debt interest will erode most of your investment gains, so your spending habits are what count.

Level 2: Stability

Here your income exceeds your expenses, and you have a small amount to save regularly. Your debt is also under control. The main job on this level is the buffer. Building a starter emergency fund, and saving every month whether the month went well or not. I'd argue this is the level that matters most. The buffer is what stops one bad event (a boiler, a lost job) from knocking you back down to Survival. Everything above this level rests on it.

Level 3: Surplus

Your income comfortably exceeds your expenses, you're saving and investing, the right insurance is in place, debt is low, net worth is positive. Here the goal moves from stability to growth. Investing starts to matter more than saving, because at this level your savings rate has done most of what it can. This is also where you can start looking at passive income streams.

Level 4: Security

Everything above, plus a proper emergency fund, diversified assets, protection for your family, no or very low consumer debt, a real retirement plan, passive income that covers all or most of your expenses, your estate in order. The job here is to maintain what you have, so you don't slip back to the levels below.

What to do next? Well, find your level honestly, then do the job for that level and ignore the jobs above it. I think the most common mistake is doing Level 3 jobs while standing on Level 1. Trying to pick funds while the credit card grows.

And the levels are not permanent. A redundancy or an illness can drop you one, sometimes two. That is not failure. It is just where you are now, and the same rule applies: do the job for the level you're on. In other words, you need to gamify your finances.

As you get older, this matters more. There is less time to recover from mistakes, and usually more people counting on you.
