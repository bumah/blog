Lately, I've become somewhat obsessed with longevity. It all started when I noticed my blood pressure was higher than normal. (PS: if you are over 40 and don't regularly check your blood pressure, you should do that now.) That sent me reading about heart health, how to age better, and I haven't really stopped since. The news lately seems full of billionaires and Silicon Valley companies working on ways to reverse ageing. The topic kept finding me (and my social media feeds). I'm sort of geeky, so once something has my attention I want to understand it properly. The more I read about this topic the more I wonder why I didn't pay attention to it earlier.

I'll start by explaining what longevity really means to me. When people hear "longevity" they picture living to 100, or a billionaire trying to hack his way to 200. For me, it is different. Longevity is about the quantity of quality years.

We are already winning the living-long part. A century ago, average life expectancy was somewhere in the 30s and 40s. Today it is around 70+ globally, and low 80s in a lot of developed countries. We've added decades to our lifespan, but how are those extra decades actually spent?

There's a word for this: healthspan. Lifespan is how long you live. Healthspan is how long you stay healthy in those years. Active and sharp, able to do the things you enjoy without your body holding you back. And there is a gap between the two. On average, people spend roughly the last decade of life in poor health, managing chronic conditions rather than living freely. So you might get to 80, but the last 10 of those years aren't exactly the good times.

What's both encouraging and sad is that most of what drives that gap (heart disease, type 2 diabetes, etc.) comes down to everyday choices we can actually do something about. It is not all fixed at birth. My blood pressure told me that much. That reframed the whole thing for me. Push my healthspan as close to lifespan as I can, so the extra time is time worth having.

There's also a second part I think gets left out of most longevity conversation: money. Living a long, healthy life is great right up until you run out of cash to actually enjoy it. That's your wealthspan. How long your money lasts, and whether it keeps up with a longer life. If we're all living into our 80s and beyond, our retirement savings and income have to stretch a lot further than previous generations planned for. Being fit and broke at 80 isn't exactly living the dream.

So for me, longevity is really three things: lifespan, healthspan and wealthspan.

A lot of this depends on decisions that compound over time. So the earlier you start the better. I'm going to write about what I'm actually doing to extend my longevity: the training, the food, the things worth watching, the money, the tools I'm using, and what I'm learning as I go. Partly to keep myself honest, and also because if it helps even one person to start sooner, good.
