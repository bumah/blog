Many people dream of living longer. Living long is great until the money runs out.

I've written a few posts about the health side of longevity: what I do to keep fit, manage my heart health and mental wellbeing. But there's a whole other half which matters just as much. Money. To be specific, having enough money to actually enjoy a long life.

This is called your wealthspan. Living to 90 or 100 only works if you can stay healthy enough to do the things you enjoy and afford a comfortable lifestyle.

A lot of people reach their 40s, 50s and 60s with nowhere near enough saved to cover decades of not working during retirement. Most people are counting on the state pension to carry them through. BUT that's a BIG risk. Governments are extending retirement ages, meaning people have to work longer, and with inflation rising there's no guarantee that what you get at 67 or 70 will be enough to sustain your lifestyle.

And it gets worse. We're living longer, so the savings have to stretch across more years than any generation before us planned for. A retirement that used to last 15 years might now need to last 30. Double the years, from the same pot.

The biggest mistake most people make is starting too late. And almost everyone starts too late, because when you're young, retirement is too far away to feel real.

The reason timing matters so much is compounding. Money you invest early grows on top of its own growth, over and over, for decades. So the years matter more than the amounts. Rough numbers: someone who starts putting a bit away at 25 can end up with around double what someone starting at 35 has by retirement, even though they only saved for ten more years. Ten early years can be worth more than everything the late starter ever puts in. Time is doing more of the work than the amounts are. And time is the one thing you cannot buy back later.

I've written a post on financial freedom which you can find here. The first step is identifying which level you're on, then making the decisions that move you up. This matters most for those still in the pre-retirement phase, with enough time left to build wealth.

For those already close to retirement, where time is not on your side, one thing to strongly consider is retirement arbitrage. Most retirement decisions are emotional. Retirement arbitrage means you pick the retirement location that best matches your financial and health needs first, not emotional security. You change the priorities. So you retire somewhere your money simply goes further. A pension that would barely cover the basics in London or New York can mean a comfortable, even generous life somewhere with a much lower cost of living, such as Peru or Thailand.

And a lot of those places happen to be good for your healthspan too. Warm enough to be outdoors all year, fresher food, a slower pace, less day-to-day stress. The places famous for long-lived populations tend to look like this. So the right move can stretch your money and your health at the same time.

Living long can be great, but you need to be intentional about your financial future to really enjoy it. Otherwise, it can become a burden for you and for the people who matter most to you.
