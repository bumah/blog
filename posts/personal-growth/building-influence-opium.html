Influence has a bad reputation, mostly because people confuse it with manipulation. But influence is just leverage — the ability to move things in a direction you believe in. And in any environment, that leverage comes from a small number of places.

There are five. Almost nobody has all of them. Look closely at the most influential people in the world and you'll find they each dominate one or two, and let those pull the rest toward them. I call the five OPIUM.

O — Opportunity

Some people's power is that they can change your life with a single yes. Jay-Z can pluck an unknown artist and turn them into a household name. A director like Scorsese or Spielberg can cast someone nobody's heard of and make them a star overnight. Oprah could put a book on her list and mint a bestseller. When you control access to the opportunities people badly want, everyone trying to rise has to come through you. You're the door — and that's influence.

P — People

Other people's power is simply who they know. This is the currency of politics: a president isn't influential because of what they can personally do, but because of the coalition they can pick up the phone and move. The best-connected person in any industry makes things happen without lifting anything themselves — one introduction, one favour called in. Your network is borrowed leverage, and the people who build it deliberately end up at the centre of everything.

I — Information

Then there are people whose edge is knowing what others don't. Michael Bloomberg built a fortune — and a political career — selling the financial world the information it couldn't operate without. Warren Buffett's advantage was never a secret formula; it was reading more, and more carefully, than everyone else. The person with the clearest picture of what's really going on quietly shapes decisions everyone else is only guessing at.

U — Utility

The most honest form of influence is being irreplaceably good. LeBron James and Cristiano Ronaldo don't need anyone's permission — they are the value, and entire teams, leagues and businesses rearrange themselves around them. Sam Altman's pull in Silicon Valley came from years of being useful — building, advising, running Y Combinator — until he was wired into everything. When you're the best at something people need, you can't be ignored. You matter because you actually deliver.

M — Money

And then there's the bluntest lever of all. When Elon Musk wants something built, bought, or changed, capital makes it move — companies, rockets, an entire social network bought outright. Command over resources — capital, budget, who gets paid — is the most transferable kind of power there is. It isn't the deepest form of influence, and rarely the most admired, but pretending it isn't power is naive.

The point

Notice the pattern: dominate one pillar and it tends to pull the others in. Jay-Z turned opportunity into money. Athletes turn utility into a network. Politicians trade people for information and back again.

So you don't have to be born rich or connected. Pick the pillar you can realistically own — the skill you can become the best at, the information you can master, the people you can bring together — and go deep. Influence isn't a personality you're born with. It's leverage, and these are the five places it comes from.
