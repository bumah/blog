As a kid, you did all of this without trying. You kept your little promises, ran on habit, chased what you loved, learned something new every day, and did the chores you were told to. Somewhere along the way it got complicated. But your life is still just your days, repeated — and every day is made of the same handful of things. When I look at what actually fills a day, it comes down to five, and they spell CHILD: Commitments, Habits, Ikigai, Learn, Duties. Living like a child again is just doing these five on purpose.

C — Commitments

The things you choose to take on. These are your call — the promises you make because you decided they matter. The training plan you signed up for, the side project you swore you'd finish, the weekly call with an old friend, showing up for bedtime no matter how the day went. Nobody forces these on you, which is exactly why they count. Honour the ones you make, and get pickier about which ones you make.

H — Habits

The things you do without deciding. The morning walk, leaving your phone out of the bedroom, the drink you're trying to cut from most nights. Your habits run most of your day whether you notice or not, and over time they quietly become you. You don't rise to your goals; you fall to your habits — so it's worth building the ones you'd be happy to be defined by.

I — Ikigai

Your reason for being — the thing that gets you up and gives the day a point. Sometimes it's the business you're building, sometimes it's the people you're raising, sometimes it's a craft you lose hours in. It doesn't have to be grand, but it has to be real and it has to be yours. Tend it a little every day and the years stop feeling like they're just passing.

L — Learn

Learn something every day. A page of a book, a skill for the next chapter, how to invest properly, or just letting someone younger teach you something. It doesn't have to be big — a mistake understood counts. The trap is thinking you already know how things work; that's exactly when you stop growing. A day with no learning in it is a day you didn't grow.

D — Duties

The things you must do. The bills, the tax return, the school run, the family that needs you. Duties don't get a vote — they're the unglamorous stuff that holds everything up. They aren't exciting, but doing them well is the foundation the other four stand on. Handle what you must, so you're free to enjoy what you choose.

These five are just the raw material of a day. Live them on purpose — commitments kept, habits chosen, ikigai tended, something learned, duties done — and you've had a good day. String enough good days together and you've had a good life.
