I've been thinking a lot lately about how I want to live the next part of my life.

I turned 40, and the truth is the last year or two have been some of the most defining of my life — a lot of change, personal and professional, arriving all at once. Without wanting to sound dramatic, it feels like the decisions I've made over these last twelve to eighteen months are the ones that will set my trajectory for the decades ahead. That kind of realisation makes you stop and pay attention.

Around the same time, I came across a quote that stayed with me. The idea was simple: life is about moments and memories, and if you don't take the moments, you don't get the memories. Something about it landed. I've since adapted it into my own version:

Life is about taking the moments and building the memories that make you feel truly ALIVE.

And, being who I am, I couldn't leave it there. I wanted a way to actually live that idea rather than just admire it on a screen. So I turned it into a framework — five pillars to help me get the balance right and build a life that's rich, meaningful, and genuinely enjoyable. It spells the word itself. ALIVE.

Aspirations. Love. Interests. Vitality. Explorations.

What I like about it, and the reason it works for me now in a way a simpler list wouldn't have at 22 or 32, is that each pillar isn't just a category. It's a shift — a change in how I relate to that part of life now that I'm older. The pillars haven't changed much over the years. My relationship to them has changed completely.

A is for Aspirations — the shift from ambition to purpose

At 22, or even 32, my aspirations were mostly about status. The next title, the bigger salary, the line on the resume. Ambition pointed outward, at how things looked.

At 40 I feel that pull loosening, and something quieter taking its place. The question isn't "how do I climb higher?" anymore. It's "what is all this energy actually for?" What do I want to leave behind? What's the something-bigger worth pouring myself into? Ambition asks what you can get. Purpose asks what you can serve. That's the shift, and it changes everything downstream.

L is for Love — the shift from quantity to depth

For a long time I measured my social world by its size. More contacts, more network, more people in the room.

Now I care far less about the number and far more about the depth. This is the season to audit your relationships honestly and pour your energy into the ones that are real — the family, the friends, the handful of people who genuinely make you feel safe and seen. A wide circle is easy to accumulate and easy to mistake for connection. A few deep bonds are what actually hold a life together. I'd rather have five of those than five hundred of the other.

I is for Interests — the shift from distraction to mastery

It's never been easier to fill every spare minute with something passive. Endless scrolling, endless watching, entertainment that asks nothing of you and leaves nothing behind.

The shift here is from consuming to doing. From distraction to curiosity. It means picking up the hobbies and skills I quietly shelved years ago — the instrument, the language, the craft — and giving myself permission to be a beginner again, badly, without needing to be good at it straight away. There's a particular aliveness in learning something new as an adult that passive entertainment can never give you. It's the difference between filling time and using it.

V is for Vitality — the shift from aesthetics to longevity

When I was younger, fitness was mostly about how I looked. Vanity, if I'm honest.

At 40, energy has quietly become my most valuable currency — and the way I think about my body has flipped completely. It's no longer about aesthetics. It's about longevity: eating, moving, and sleeping not to look a certain way, but to feel sharp, resilient, and capable of everything the other pillars ask of me. You can't pursue a purpose, show up for the people you love, or explore the world from a body you've let run down. Vitality is the platform the whole framework stands on. Look after it, and everything else becomes possible.

E is for Explorations — the shift from tourism to transformation

I used to think of exploring as travel — new places, photos, ticking countries off a list. Tourism.

The shift is toward experiences that actually change how I see the world. Sometimes that's still travel, but the point isn't the destination — it's coming back slightly different from how I left. And it doesn't have to involve a passport. Exploring a new philosophy, a new way of thinking, a new discipline or spiritual path can transform you just as much as a foreign city. Tourism shows you somewhere new. Transformation makes you someone new. That's what I'm after now.

Living it, not admiring it

That's ALIVE. Five pillars, five shifts — from ambition to purpose, quantity to depth, distraction to mastery, aesthetics to longevity, tourism to transformation. Together they're my attempt to make sure this next chapter is one I actually live, rather than one that simply happens to me.

I don't think you're meant to fix all five at once. The way I'm approaching it is to pick one — the pillar that feels most neglected right now — and give it my attention for a while before moving to the next. Small, deliberate steps beat a grand overhaul you can't sustain.

So if any of this resonates, that's the question I'd leave you with — the same one I'm asking myself. Of the five, which one needs your attention most right now? Your aspirations, and the search for a deeper purpose? Your love, and the relationships worth deepening? Your interests, waiting to be revived? Your vitality, and the energy underneath everything? Or your explorations, and the pull toward something that might change you?

Whichever one it is — start there. Because in the end, life really is just a collection of moments and memories. And the moments are the ones we choose to take.
