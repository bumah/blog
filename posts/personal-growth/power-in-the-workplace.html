A lot of people want to become more powerful at work — more say, more influence, a faster climb. Over the years I've noticed five things that quietly and gradually build that power: they help you influence decisions and move up the ranks without ever looking like you're grabbing for it. They spell POWER: Presence, Opinions, Work, Exposure, Relationships. Build these and you stop waiting for a promotion to have a real say.

P — Presence

How and where you present yourself matters. You can't be everywhere — and you shouldn't try to be. Anything that's always around stops getting valued; the person in every meeting and every thread becomes background noise. Presence works the other way: be deliberate about where you show up, so that when you do, it counts. Pick the rooms that matter, speak in the ones that decide things, and carry yourself well when you're there. A little scarcity, plus showing up sharp, is what makes your presence felt.

O — Opinions

Learn to speak, and learn to say what you think clearly. The person who can turn a messy problem into three clean sentences shapes the decision; the one who stays quiet gets talked over, however good their ideas are. You don't have to be the loudest in the room — you have to have a view and be able to explain it. Work on articulating your opinions until people start turning to you to make sense of things.

W — Work

This is the foundation the rest sits on. Be dependable — the person who does what they said they'd do. Be indispensable — genuinely great at something the team can't easily replace. Everything else is noise if the work isn't there; presence and exposure only help once there's something real behind them. Get excellent at one thing, and be reliable about all of it.

E — Exposure

Great work done in the dark stays in the dark. The right people — the ones who decide promotions, projects and pay — need to know what you do and that you did it. That's not bragging; it's making sure your work reaches the people it should. Volunteer for the visible project, share your results, get in front of the decision-makers. Being good isn't enough if nobody who matters knows it.

R — Relationships

Almost everything at work moves through people who trust you. The favour done quickly, the heads-up before a decision, the name put forward when you're not in the room — all of it runs on relationships. Build them widely and genuinely — up, down and sideways — before you need them. Networking isn't schmoozing; it's being someone people are glad to help.

Power isn't a dirty word. It's just the ability to get good things done. Titles come and go, but Presence, Opinions, Work, Exposure and Relationships travel with you — build the five and you carry your power wherever you go.
