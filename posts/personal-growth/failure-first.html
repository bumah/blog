Success starts by not failing.

Most advice on success tells you to visualise your end goal, plan your path, work hard, stay consistent. Plenty of people have achieved success that way. But having worked in banking and risk management for decades, I've come to believe there's an alternative that puts the odds more in your favour.

I'll get a little bit geeky for a second. In probability theory, success is just one minus failure. S = 1 − F. If an event has an 80% chance of succeeding, it has a 20% chance of failing. So if you drive the chance of failure down, the chance of success goes up by exactly the same amount. Instead of straining toward the win, I work on removing the ways it could go wrong.

For most things in life, there are more ways to succeed than ways things could go wrong. Usually it's a handful of things that could derail you. If you're trying to lose weight, for example, the main one is eating badly.

So how do you apply this? Before you start any goal, imagine it's over and the thing has already failed. Then work backwards and ask why. Flipping the timeline like this shows you risks you simply do not see when you're only picturing success.

Risk management in banking is where I first properly used this. You don't run a bank by hoping things go well. You assume losses will happen and map where they'll come from. Then you put controls in front of the biggest ones. Same instinct, applied to anything you're trying to pull off.

For each thing that could derail your goal, ask one more question: how do I make it less likely? The aim is to cut the odds, not eliminate them completely. If you can eliminate, fine. But cutting is enough. With weight loss, that could mean removing the bad food from your home rather than focusing on eating healthy. Small difference. But if the unhealthy stuff is not there to eat, your chances of eating better just went up.

Investing is another good example. Instead of chasing massive returns, apply the same thinking and you realise the top reasons you could fail are not having the right exit strategy, a proper risk assessment, and diversification. Address those three first and your chances of success improve significantly. You could make 70% gains, but if you don't exit at the right time, it can all go back to zero.

That's it. You don't need a grand plan. Find the few things most likely to sink you and deal with them before they get the chance.
