Everything ends. The job you have now will end. Your health, as it is today, will end. The good market run ends. Eventually, so do we.

I do not say this to be dark. I say it because I've spent decades in banking risk, and the whole discipline rests on one assumption: things will go wrong, so prepare while they are still going right. You do not buy insurance during the flood. Nobody will sell it to you then.

Most people treat endings the opposite way. We plan as if the present is permanent. The salary will keep arriving. The knees will keep working. The parents will always be at the end of the phone. Then the ending comes, and it lands on someone who has made no preparation at all.

Preparing does not stop the ending. Nothing stops the ending. Preparing changes what the ending does to you, and to the people around you.

Some of this is paperwork. A will. Life insurance if anyone depends on your income. An emergency fund for the ending of a job. Making sure your partner knows where the accounts are and what to do with them. I wrote about this in the financial freedom post. Getting your estate in order is the last level, and most people never get to it. Not because it is hard. Because it forces you to sit with the fact that you will need it.

Some of it is not paperwork at all. If everything ends, then time with your kids at this exact age ends too. The trip you keep postponing has a last possible year, and you will not know which year that was until it has passed. This is why I wrote in the LIFE post: don't defer fun, schedule it.

There is a question I ask myself now, the same way I would ask it of a bank. If this ended tomorrow, who gets hurt, and how badly? Run it against the job. The health. The people. Wherever the answer is "badly, and they would be lost", that is where the preparation is missing.

Sometimes thinking about the end is uncomfortable. No one wants to think about losing a parent, a job, a loved one, a relationship ending, bad health. But not thinking about it, or not preparing for it, will not stop the inevitable. Sometimes the end is forced on us. Sometimes we make the decision to end. Either way, planning for that scenario puts you in a better position than not. And would you not rather be in a better position?

We've all had surprises where things ended prematurely, and in some of those we wished we had early warnings. But if you carry this mindset, you don't need early warnings to brace for impact. You stay ready for impact.

None of this stops anything from coming. It just means that when an ending arrives, it finds you ready. Or at least less unready. That is about as much control as anyone gets.
