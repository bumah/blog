I've always loved fitness. But especially the kind you do with other people.

Group workouts. Outdoor stuff. Anything where you're pushing yourself next to someone else instead of on your own.

I'm competitive. I train harder when there's someone beside me who won't stop first.

So Hyrox was always going to get me.

If you haven't heard of it, you probably will soon. Eight 1km runs, each one followed by a workout station. Eight rounds of that. Same stations, same order, everywhere in the world. My time in Hong Kong means the same as someone's in London.

Simple. Also very hard.

For a long time I didn't pay it much attention. Then I started taking my health more seriously. Training for the years ahead, not just staying more or less fit.

Once I was doing that, Hyrox stopped looking like someone else's hobby. It looked like something to actually train for.

So I signed up.

Booking the wrong race

There are two individual categories. Open, the standard one. Pro, heavier weights, for more experienced people.

Races sell out fast. When I went to book the Open, it was gone.

What was left was Doubles Pro. The heavier division, done as a pair.

A good friend of mine was up for it. He's a personal trainer. So we booked it together.

I'm reasonably fit. I figured it would be fine.

One problem. I'm not a runner.

I'm heavy. Long-distance running has never been my thing. Give me 5km and I'll do it in 28 to 35 minutes, depending on the day. But Hyrox is 8km, with stations in between that wreck your legs.

The running was where I'd struggle.

Training, and a plan

We started training together. It felt good.

Hyrox is really two problems. The running, and the stations. Between us, the stations were fine.

So we built the plan around the running. Go easy on the runs. Steady pace. Don't blow up early. Then move fast through the stations to make up time.

We gave ourselves 90 minutes. Reasonable, we thought. Two fit guys with a plan.

Then we flew to Indonesia for the race.

Race day

As soon as we walked in, I could feel it. People of every age and size, all going as hard as they could, a crowd shouting them on.

From the outside it almost looked easy. Even though you could see plenty of them really struggling.

Our race was at 7:30pm. We got to the warm-up area around 6:45 to move a bit.

That's when I got nervous. All around us were men with their shirts off, lean and jumpy, bouncing on their toes.

Then our slot came. The horn went, and we were off.

Where it started to go wrong

The first run and the SkiErg were fine. Lap two was okay too. But we'd already run 2km, and it was starting to feel real.

Then the Sled Push. Around 200kg to drive down the track. He went first, I went second. Afterwards I was completely gassed.

The problem was he kept setting off on the next run too fast. That irritated me. So I told him, nicely, to slow down.

Then the Sled Pull. 150kg, dragging it back hand over hand. Fine in itself. But again I went second, blowing hard, and again he shot off ahead of me.

This time I was properly annoyed. I said something, less nicely. He eased off.

Then the Burpee Broad Jumps. They spike your heart rate more than anything else in the race. After that, the Row.

I said I'd go first this time.

That row was the longest rest I got all race. Two minutes to actually breathe while he pulled. Somewhere in there I started enjoying it again.

Here's the part I'm not proud of. Now he was the one gassed.

Watching him struggle for once felt good. I won't pretend otherwise.

But something changed after that. We stopped competing with each other and started working together.

Finishing

The back half went better. Farmers Carry. Sandbag Lunges. Then Wall Balls. The ones that come when you've already run 6 or 7km.

We got through them next to each other.

We crossed the line at 94 minutes. Four minutes over. It felt great anyway.

Looking back, the four minutes don't bother me.

What stuck was laps three and four. They were the worst of it. Gassed, annoyed, my partner pulling away up the track. Somewhere in there I decided I wasn't going to make it.

Then the row gave me two minutes standing still. By the next lap I felt almost fine.

We plan on doing this again. This time with a better running base. And sticking to the Pros.

If you've been thinking about signing up, I'd just do it. I booked the wrong race by accident, and I'm still glad I did.
