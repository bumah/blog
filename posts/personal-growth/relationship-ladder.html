A few years ago I moved to Hong Kong. It's a city with a revolving door built into it. People arrive, stay a few years, and leave, and you learn quickly not to assume anyone is permanent. Building deep relationships here is hard, and it gets harder as you get older, when most people already have their circle and less time to add to it.

That's what got me thinking about this properly. Deep connections take real time and energy, and both get scarcer with age. So you have to be deliberate about where you spend them. I didn't want to keep pouring myself into relationships that were never going anywhere, and I didn't want to neglect the few that could actually go deep.

So I started paying attention to what actually holds a relationship together. Not just romance. Friendships, family, colleagues, business partners. The same handful of things came up every time. Six of them, and the more of them a relationship has, the stronger and deeper it gets.

One thing I noticed early. Most people judge a relationship by its best moment, the great conversation, the one time someone came through. But the best moment tells you almost nothing about whether something lasts. What matters is how many of the six are actually there.

The idea

The more of these six a relationship has, the stronger it is. Look at which ones are there, and which ones aren't yet.

The six pillars

1. Expectations. This is the one I've seen wreck more relationships than any other, and usually the quietest. Two people want different things from the same relationship and never say so. One is casual, the other is invested. One wants a business partner, the other wants a friend who happens to do business. Nothing is wrong with either person. They're just not in the same relationship, and no amount of energy or effort fixes a mismatch nobody named out loud. When I feel friction with someone now, the first thing I check is whether we ever actually agreed, even silently, on what this is.

2. Energy. How do I feel around this person? Do I leave lighter, or drained? Some people I can be tired walking in and still come away better. Others I dread before I've even seen them. Energy is the day-to-day texture of being together, and it's usually the first thing you feel and the last thing you can argue yourself out of.

3. Effort. Good feeling isn't enough. Do they show up, keep their word, make the time? I've had friendships full of warmth that slowly died because I was always the one reaching out. Relationships run on consistent effort, not good intentions.

4. Esteem. Do I genuinely respect who this person is: their character, their values, how they treat people? This is the one that quietly ends a lot of old friendships. I have childhood and university friends I once felt inseparable from, and nothing dramatic ever happened between us. We just grew up, and somewhere along the way I stopped respecting how one or two of them lived, or they stopped respecting me. The warmth was still there. The esteem wasn't, and once that goes the rest doesn't hold for long.

5. Emotional Safety. Can I be myself? Disagree without it becoming a problem? The relationships I trust most are the ones where I don't edit myself before I speak. If I'm walking on eggshells, something is already wrong, however good it looks from the outside.

6. Endurance. Can it survive time and difficulty? Hard seasons, distance, both people changing? This is what separates a good moment from something you can actually build on, and you only really learn it when things get hard.

The more you have, the stronger it is

I don't treat these as a pass-or-fail test. It's more that each one a relationship has adds to how much weight it can carry. One or two, and it's pleasant but light. Four or five genuinely there, and it can hold real life: hard seasons, distance, disagreement, time.

They're also a rough measure of how deep a relationship really is. Most start with one or two. You meet someone, the energy is good, but there's no effort or endurance yet because there's been no time. As it grows, the Es come online one by one. Expectations get aligned, effort becomes reliable, esteem builds, safety settles in, and endurance gets proven by surviving something hard.

So when I'm trying to work out where I stand with someone, I count the Es. The more of them are genuinely there, the more solid the relationship is. A new friendship with three is promising. An old one that's quietly lost its esteem or its effort is thinner than it looks. You can watch a relationship gaining Es or shedding them, and that tells you far more than any single good or bad day ever will.

Then: improve or adapt

When one of the six is clearly missing, there are really only two honest moves.

Improve it. If both people want to, and it's the kind of thing that can change. Effort, expectations and energy often can, if you actually talk about them.

Adapt to what's there. If it won't change, stop asking the relationship to be something it can't. A colleague low on emotional safety can still be a great collaborator, just not someone you confide in. A friend low on effort is someone you enjoy when you see them, not someone you lean on. That's not a failure. It just has a ceiling on how far it goes, and the peace comes from accepting where it is.

The one thing to take away

The way I've come to see it, there's no such thing as a bad relationship. There are just different types, sitting at different depths. When I look at how many of the six a relationship has, I'm not grading the person. I'm working out what kind of relationship this actually is, and how far it's likely to go.

The older I get, the more that matters. Time and energy for people are limited, and they don't refill. This is really just a way of spending them where they count: building the few relationships that can go deep, and making peace with the ones that were only ever built to hold a couple of the six.
