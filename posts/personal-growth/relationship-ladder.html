Most people judge a relationship by its best moment — the great conversation, the one time someone came through. But a relationship isn't held up by its best moment. It's held up by six pillars, and it's only as strong as the weakest one.

This isn't only about romance. The same six pillars hold up a friendship, a family tie, a working partnership, or a business relationship. When something feels off and you can't quite name why, it's usually one of these missing.

The idea

Find the pillar that's missing — then either build it, or build around it.

The six pillars

1. Expectations. Every relationship rests on aligned expectations. Do you both want the same kind of thing from it? Mismatched expectations — one person casual, one invested; one wanting a partner, the other a friend — sink a relationship no matter how good everything else is.

2. Energy. How do you feel around this person? Do you leave lighter, or drained? Energy is the day-to-day texture of being together.

3. Effort. Good feeling isn't enough. Do they show up, keep their word, make time? Relationships run on consistent effort, not good intentions.

4. Esteem. Do you genuinely respect who they are — their character, their values, how they treat people? Warmth without respect doesn't last.

5. Emotional Safety. Can you be yourself? Disagree without fear? A good relationship is a place of peace, not one that keeps you anxious.

6. Endurance. Can you build something over time — navigate hard seasons, grow, keep choosing it? This is what separates a moment from a foundation.

It's only as strong as its weakest pillar

Don't average the pillars. A relationship's ceiling is set by its weakest one, not its best. Five strong pillars don't make up for a missing sixth.

Find what's missing

What each pillar looks like when it's holding, and when it isn't:

PillarStrong looks likeWeak looks like
ExpectationsYou both want the same thing from itYou keep wanting different things from it
EnergyYou leave lighter than you cameYou dread the interaction more than you enjoy it
EffortYou both reach outYou're always the one reaching out
EsteemYou respect how they liveYou like them, but wouldn't point to how they live
Emotional SafetyYou can be fully yourselfYou edit yourself, walk on eggshells
EnduranceIt holds when things get hardIt works when easy, breaks the moment it's hard

Then: improve or adapt

Once you've found the weak pillar, there are only two honest moves.

Improve it — if both people want to, and it's the kind of thing that can change. Effort, expectations and energy often can.

Adapt to the ceiling — if it won't change, stop asking the relationship to be something it can't. A colleague who's low on emotional safety can still be a great collaborator, just not a confidant. A charming friend low on effort is someone you enjoy, not someone you rely on. The relationship isn't a failure. It just has a ceiling, and peace comes from accepting it.

The one thing to take away

The way I see it, there's no such thing as a bad relationship. There are just different types. When you find the weakest pillar, you're not grading the person — you're learning what kind of relationship this actually is. A friend low on effort isn't a bad friend, they're a different kind of friend. A colleague low on emotional safety isn't a bad colleague, they're a working relationship, not a confidant.

The mistake is expecting one type to behave like another. Recognise the type of relationship you're in, and work by those rules. That's where the peace is — not in trying to fix everyone into something they're not, but in seeing clearly and stopping paying full price for a relationship that can't hold what you keep handing it.
