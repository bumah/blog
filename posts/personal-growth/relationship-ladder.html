Most people ask the wrong question when they meet someone: "Could this person be my husband or wife?" It's a natural question, but it's the wrong one.

A better question is: "What kind of relationship is this person actually suited for?"

The answer isn't based on one feeling, one good date, or one moment of chemistry. It's based on the ingredients they consistently bring into your life over time. I call this the 6E Relationship Ladder.

Unlike a traditional ladder, this isn't about racing from one rung to the next. It's a way to discover what kind of relationship someone is capable of sustaining. Every new E builds on the one before it. You don't stop checking attraction because you've found emotional safety. You don't ignore effort because you admire someone's character. Each level assumes all the previous boxes are still checked.

1. Expectations

Every relationship begins with attraction — looks, chemistry, personality, first impressions. The only question at this stage is: "Am I interested enough to get to know this person?"

This isn't love. It's just the door opening.

2. Energy

As you spend more time together, you discover how they make you feel. Do you look forward to seeing them? Do conversations flow? Do they bring excitement and warmth into your life, or do they leave you drained?

Attraction gets you through the door. Energy decides whether you want to come back.

3. Effort

Chemistry is effortless. Relationships aren't. This is where you find out whether they genuinely invest in you. Do they make time? Do they communicate? Do they keep their word? Do they choose you consistently?

Relationships aren't built on good intentions. They're built on consistent effort.

4. Esteem

Now you begin to see who they really are. Beyond the attraction and the fun, do you actually admire this person? Do you respect their character, their values, their integrity? Would you be proud to build a future alongside someone like them?

Love without respect rarely lasts.

5. Emotional Safety

This is where the relationship becomes a place of peace. Can you be yourself? Can you be vulnerable? Can you disagree without fear? Do they make your life calmer instead of more chaotic?

A great partner doesn't just make you happy. They make you feel safe.

6. Endurance

Finally comes the biggest question. Not "Can we stay together?" but "Can we build a life together?"

Endurance isn't just surviving hard times. It's building something over decades — shared values, shared vision, shared goals, shared responsibility, and the ability to grow and navigate challenges together. Marriage isn't about staying together. It's about continuing to build together.

The rule

The 6Es are cumulative. Someone ready for marriage doesn't only have Endurance — they still have everything that got them there: Expectations, Energy, Effort, Esteem, Emotional Safety, and Endurance. Every higher level assumes every previous box is still checked.

Choosing the right relationship

The point of the ladder isn't to decide whether someone is a good or bad person. It's to decide what kind of relationship they're suited for.

Not every relationship has to become a marriage. Some people are meant to be casual dates. Some become close friends. Some become wonderful long-term partners. Very few become people you can build an entire life with. The mistake many of us make is trying to force someone into a relationship they're simply not equipped for.

Imagine someone who only checks the first box. They're attractive and the chemistry is undeniable, but that's where it ends. They don't bring positive energy, they don't make consistent effort, you don't particularly admire their character, and you don't feel emotionally safe. That's not a failed relationship — it just has a different ceiling. The ladder tells you this person may be perfect for something casual, and trying to turn them into a lifelong partner will likely end in disappointment, because the ingredients aren't there.

Likewise, someone may check the first four boxes but fail the fifth. You admire them, you enjoy being with them, you respect who they are — but the relationship constantly leaves you anxious and walking on eggshells. That's a sign emotional safety is missing, and without peace, long-term commitment becomes very hard.

The framework isn't about lowering your standards. It's about matching the relationship to the evidence. Don't force someone into a role they haven't shown they can fill. Accept the relationship for what it is, not what you hope it will become. The healthiest relationships aren't built on potential. They're built on proven ingredients.

Final thought

The strongest relationships aren't built on a single quality. They're built on six. Looks attract. Energy connects. Effort builds. Esteem deepens. Safety strengthens. Endurance creates a future.

Don't decide the relationship first. Discover the ingredients first — then choose the relationship they're truly suited for.
